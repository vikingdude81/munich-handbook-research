The verb-preposition combinations with special idiomatic meanings ("collocations" as they are known
in linguistics) have not received the same attention in Egyptology as they have in the study of other
languages, because Egyptology was and is occupied with sentence-theory. Take for example the
English collocation (verb + preposition in function of an adverb) "live on" as an expression of the
stable diet that a living being eats. Here we can say "The goats live on grass" or "He lives on bread" to
express that they eat this substance to stay alive, which is different from "The goats live on the hill"
and "he lives on the street", as the preposition "on" in these sentences is not a part of a collocation, but
a part of the prepositional phrase (cf. also "A boy looked through the keyhole" and "A manager looked
through the files").

Such collocations also existed in Egyptian, and the preposition used in them changed the verb’s
meaning just like a preposition in the function of an adverb does in English:
